Case study · 01

23 animated icons in about an hour, with AI

Each hover above is a small micro-interaction: gears that mesh, a crane that swings, a trowel that smooths concrete away. None of it was hand-animated. It came out of a short back-and-forth between a designer's eye and an AI agent, with the original artwork kept intact.

How it came together

Four versions, each one fixing what the last one got wrong.

  1. 01

    Redraw and animate

    The AI rebuilt every icon as SVG so its parts could move on their own: fan blades spin, the hammer strikes, the trees sway.

    Lively motionIcons redrawn

  2. 02

    Keep the original artwork

    The icons were cut from the screenshot pixel for pixel, but a flat image can only move as a whole: it spins, bounces or shakes.

    Exact iconsMotion felt static

  3. 03

    Cut the artwork into layers

    Each icon was split into its natural parts (gears, forks, the wrecking ball), with every pixel on exactly one layer. At rest the layers rebuild the original exactly; on hover they move independently.

    Exact iconsLively motion

  4. 04

    Direct a single moment

    With the system working, one icon got a precise brief. The trowel sweeps across the splash, and a slanted mask riding along the blade's edge makes the splash vanish under it, then reappear on the way back.

    Storytelling detail

The prompts

Plain language, written the way you'd talk to a teammate. Lightly trimmed for reading.

What made it fast

The AI did the heavy lifting. Design judgement steered it.

  • Start from a picture

    One screenshot carried layout, colour, type and all 23 icons. No spec document needed.

  • React in plain words

    “It looks very static” was enough feedback to change the whole approach.

  • Point back at what worked

    Naming the earlier version as the reference saved re-explaining the motion style.

  • Make constraints testable

    “Don't change the illustration” turned into a check: zero pixel difference at rest.

  • Give motion meaning

    Gears turn, a ball swings, heat rises. Each animation says something about the trade.

  • Respect the rest state

    Icons finish their loop when the cursor leaves, work with the keyboard, and stay still for reduced-motion users.

Under the hood

Plain web tech, no animation library.

  • CSS keyframesOne small motion per part
  • SVG masksFor the trowel's wipe effect
  • ~20 lines of JSStart on hover, finish the loop on leave

Case study · 02

From a plain avatar to an orb people notice

The chatbot on the current site sits behind a small, flat green circle. It looks like every other help widget, so most visitors scroll straight past it. We replaced it with a living orb and a friendly face that shows what the bot is doing, so the chat earns attention instead of waiting for it.

Why change the avatar

The old avatar was easy to ignore. The new one is hard to miss, without being loud.

How it came together

Five steps, each one shaped by what the last one showed.

  1. 01

    Bring the orb in

    An existing swirling orb was rebuilt in plain CSS for this page, and a colour bug in the original was fixed along the way.

    Rich motionBuilt for dark backgrounds

  2. 02

    Make it at home on white

    The stage went white, and the vignette, rim shadow and glow layers that made the edges look dark were retuned for a light page.

    Clean on white

  3. 03

    Put it to work in the chat

    The chatbot was recreated from a screen recording, with the orb as the bot's avatar and launcher. The first pass flickered on every word; the feedback asked for a smooth pulse instead.

    In contextFlicker felt nervous

  4. 04

    Give each state its own look

    Idle has no glow. Thinking glows steadily. Answering pulses in step with the words as they appear, then settles back to rest.

    States you can read

  5. 05

    Add a face and a name

    The brand's speech-bubble mascot, inverted to white, sits on the orb. Its eyes swap places along a figure-eight while thinking and drift gently while answering, always inside the bubble. The bot became B-BOT.

    Personality

The prompts

Six of the eleven, lightly trimmed. A screen recording and an image did a lot of the explaining.

Why an orb works

The reasoning behind replacing a static icon with something alive.

  • Motion gets noticed

    People notice movement before they read anything. A slow drift pulls the eye to the chat without shouting.

  • It shows the bot is working

    Glow and pulse turn waiting into feedback: you can see it thinking, then answering.

  • A face invites a question

    The mascot makes the bot feel like someone to talk to, not a support form.

  • Still on brand

    The site's own greens, a light background and the brand's bubble shape keep it familiar.

  • Calm by default

    No glow at rest. Motion only ramps up when something is actually happening.

  • Works for everyone

    It works with a keyboard and screen readers, and stays still for people who prefer reduced motion.
